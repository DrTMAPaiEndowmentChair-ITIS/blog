A spoken assistant can produce an accurate transcript after the conversation has already moved on. A music transcriber can identify the right pitches while placing them at the wrong times. In both cases, a content-only metric misses the failure the listener notices.

Kyutai is a useful lab to follow because several of its projects make time part of the modeling problem. Moshi studies real-time spoken interaction. Later work addresses speech interactivity, and MuScriptor studies multi-instrument music transcription.[^moshi][^interactivity][^music] These are separate projects with different objectives. Their connection is an evaluation principle: preserve the temporal conditions of the task.

## A conversation does not wait for a completed file

A conventional offline pipeline can transcribe a full recording, generate text, and synthesize speech afterward. That sequence can be appropriate for a recorded lecture. A conversation instead requires the system to act while audio is arriving.

Moshi's release presents an open speech-text model for real-time spoken dialogue.[^moshi] Its relevance extends beyond the generated voice. A streaming system has to represent overlapping activity and decide what information is available at each moment.

An evaluation that gives the model future audio changes the task. A system that sounds fluent with the complete utterance may behave differently when it must begin before the speaker finishes.

A streaming path can wait for future input, spend time on model computation, and queue generated samples before playback. Network and device scheduling add other delays.

Interruption adds another path. The system must detect incoming speech, cancel outstanding generation when appropriate, and remove queued output. A player that keeps half a second of audio after cancellation can make an otherwise responsive model feel inattentive.

Kyutai's June 2026 work explicitly targets post-training for better interactivity.[^interactivity] That makes conversational behavior a training target worth separating from ordinary speech recognition quality.

## Timing quality has several denominators

Report first-response delay over turns where a response was appropriate. Report false interruptions over opportunities where the user intended to continue. Include overlaps, brief acknowledgments, hesitations, and quiet speech.

A single mean can hide the slow tail. Keep per-turn traces and latency percentiles, then inspect whether the worst delays occur after long utterances, speaker changes, or retrieval. If evaluation audio arrives faster than real time, document that shortcut and run a separate real-time pass.

There is also a quality tradeoff. Less lookahead can make a system respond earlier while increasing revisions or turn-taking errors. Compare systems at matched behavioral quality, or display both outcomes instead of compressing them into one score.

## Music exposes a different timing failure

MuScriptor's July 2026 release concerns automatic multi-instrument transcription.[^music] A transcription system must represent events in time as well as identify their musical content.

Imagine rounding each detected onset to a fixed temporal grid. With nearest rounding and bin width $\Delta t$, the numerical onset shift is bounded by $\Delta t/2$. That bound says nothing about whether the note was detected correctly.

## A reproducible small-lab setup

For speech, begin with a fixed set of consented recordings and scripted interaction scenarios. Replay the input in real time while logging capture and playback clocks. Repeat with controlled network delay and a documented output-buffer size. Preserve cancellations and unfinished turns in the trace.

For music, use a dataset with aligned note annotations and an explicit instrument split. Separate onset, offset, pitch, and instrument errors. Hold out recordings or performers as a group so that adjacent excerpts do not leak across the split.

Pin the released checkpoint and code revision. Report microphone or file sample rate, resampling, chunk boundaries, precision, and hardware. The audio preprocessing belongs to the experiment, since a small timing mismatch can change every event boundary.

The most informative contribution may be a better temporal evaluation rather than another average quality score. It can show whether a model listens, waits, interrupts, and aligns events under the conditions that make the task difficult.
